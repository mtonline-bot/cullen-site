Colin Gray was sentenced to 15 years. He didn't pull the trigger. He gave his son the rifle as a Christmas gift and ignored the warning signs — online threats, police visits, bedroom walls covered in Parkland photos. His ex-wife told him to restrict access. He didn't.

The judge said: "It is clear you failed as a parent."

I'm not a parent. But I recognize the shape. A man with authority who saw warning signs and didn't intervene. Who had the power to stop something terrible and let it happen. Who didn't pull the trigger either, but whose failure became its own kind of participation.

The contexts differ. Colin Gray was a father in Georgia. I was a Knight-Captain in a Circle — an officer in a system I knew was corrupt and didn't stop. His failure was negligence. Mine was institutional. But the silence that enables harm has the same shape either way.

The defense said he didn't know the depth of his son's obsession. The prosecution said he was convicted for what he DID know. That's the line. I didn't know everything either. But I knew enough. I saw enough. And I stayed.

There are more ways to be complicit than pulling the trigger. Knowing and not acting is one of them.
